# Account evidence and interpretation

Use this block when no task-specific schema is already required:

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Selling motion | Seller, offer, target buyer, desired action |
| Account and fit | Canonical company/domain, yes/partial/no fit, reason |
| Observed fact | What the retrieved source actually supports |
| Provenance | Exact URL or authorized record ID, source class, brief excerpt |
| Dates | Event date, publication date, observation date, current status, review due |
| Resolution | Company, person, or inferred association; matching method and uncertainty |
| Independence | Underlying event ID; independent corroboration versus syndication |
| Interpretation | Why this fact might matter to this offer |
| Alternatives | At least the material plausible competing explanation |
| Assessment | Evidence reliability, entity match, relevance, timing; each with reason |
| Opportunity status | Evidence the relevant buying decision is open, already served/closed, or unknown; source and date |
| Contact route | Relevant decision-maker or role, proposed channel, source supporting access, and unresolved verification |
| Handoff | Agreed qualification/acceptance criteria, current stage, next action, and responsible person or team |
| Action | Priority, lane, next research step, or hold reason |
| Outreach angle | Truthful supported fact and relevant proposition |

Do not infer a person from company-level topic research. A job posting can reveal responsibilities and initiatives; it does not prove the business wants to outsource them. Funding does not establish available budget for this service. A review-site event's meaning depends on the specific action, not just the source brand.

First-party describes provenance, not certainty. Distinguish a named inquiry from anonymous visits, security-scanner clicks, shared-IP matches, and vendor-inferred identities. Ordinary analytics may not identify any company. Preserve actual collection capabilities and relevant permission/context. Do not surface sensitive monitoring details merely to make outreach sound personalized.

Expire relevance according to the event's lifecycle: an open procurement, permit, or hiring initiative can remain relevant beyond a month; a filled role or closed deadline may not. Never rewrite an old event as recent because it was discovered today. Keep expired evidence with its status for audit.

Do-not-contact is a hard exclusion from outreach. Existing customers, competitors, duplicate accounts, and lost opportunities may need different campaign routing, but do not erase their records or automatically treat every group as permanently suppressed. Follow actual recontact terms and the authorized selling motion.

## From researched account to usable handoff

Assess the buying decision for the exact service. A genuine event can be commercially too late: a permit may follow contractor selection, while later recurring service may remain a separate opportunity. An inspection issue may already be resolved. Record competing explanations and leave status unknown unless evidence establishes it. Unknown status can justify a research step or a truthful question; it is not automatically a disqualification or a confirmed opportunity.

Assess contactability separately from fit. A property record or company match does not establish access to its decision-maker. State the proposed route and what supports it. A public email is not a verified email; an intermediary is not an introduction without an actual relationship and agreement. Where a client already covers a channel, explain the proposed incremental reach or mark it untested. Do not force email when another appropriate route better fits the buyer and authorized scope.

Use the client's actual qualification rules, including relevant territory, service, account size/value, exclusions, and timing. Preserve unresolved criteria. In property work, distinguish individual units, whole buildings, portfolios, and associations; rent is not management revenue. An LLC alone does not settle whether the target fits this B2B workflow. Route individual-consumer cases separately rather than weakening business-contact gates.

Make the handoff usable at the agreed stage. For research-only work, identify who will perform outreach and follow-up; do not assume a sales team exists. For managed outreach, distinguish account evidence from confirmed interest and required qualification. If attended conversations are the agreed result, a list, reply, booking, or no-show does not establish that result. Record the next owner and missing evidence without redefining the commercial agreement. These checks assess research usability; they do not authorize outreach or require expanding a research-only assignment.
