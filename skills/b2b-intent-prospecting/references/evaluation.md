# Evaluate signal value

Define the decision first: does this research method improve qualified opportunities per account or acquisition cost enough to justify its effort?

Separate tests of account selection from tests of messaging. If both the list and angle change, call the result a comparison of two outreach packages, not causal proof of signal lift. When isolating messaging, assign comparable accounts to treatments with account-level grouping so colleagues at the same company do not contaminate variants. Record exclusions and baseline differences.

Predefine primary outcome, assignment unit, evaluation window, minimum effect worth pursuing, costs, and guardrails. A 50- or 100-account pilot can reveal feasibility and gross problems; neither that size nor a twofold rate difference alone proves superiority. Compare counts and uncertainty, and allow the expected sales cycle to mature.

Track researched accounts, usable signal coverage, strict-valid contact yield where applicable, qualified replies, attended fit calls, opportunities/wins, hours, and direct costs. Deduplicate account outcomes. Keep other campaign factors comparable when possible and record changes when not.

For paid data, check actual source coverage, resolution, refresh cadence, vendor methodology, permissions, exports, and cost from current primary documentation. Use a bounded sample if needed to evaluate that data. Existing public sources are a good economical starting point, not evidence that every paid feed is unnecessary.

If sources are sparse or outcomes immature, report what is known and the next decision point. Do not label a vendor or signal effective based only on an attractive example account.
