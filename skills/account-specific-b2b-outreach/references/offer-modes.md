# Select the paid product before writing

This is an offer-flexible writing skill, not a mandate to sell managed outreach. The prompt and relevant approved brief choose the product. Prospect research can inform customization; it does not approve new payment terms or fulfillment capabilities.

## Establish the distinction

Before writing, capture these facts briefly in the internal brief, not as a questionnaire in the email:

- Who AFT is selling to and who that company's customers are.
- What the client actually receives and what AFT operates to produce it.
- What the client still owns, such as outreach, estimates, bids, clinical judgment or closing.
- What is billable, what counts as qualified, and which payment/risk condition is approved. Unknown is an acceptable draft value.
- What the CTA asset demonstrates, whether it exists, and whether we can deliver it if requested.

Choose the actual mode, or a clearly defined combination. Do not choose by niche label alone.

| Mode | Paid work and result | Do not silently claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Managed account development | Find suitable downstream accounts, reach the appropriate people, handle follow-up, qualify interest and arrange the approved conversation. | Signed clients, revenue, attended meetings when only booking is promised, or automatic pay-only-on-results terms. |
| Opportunity research and handoffs | Identify relevant accounts, properties or projects; check public fit and timing; identify the responsible person and a usable route; hand over context the client can act on. | Prospect interest, contact already made, a booked call, a verified direct email, or client outreach operated by AFT. |
| Monitoring plus opportunity handoffs | Revisit defined source/event families, assess meaningful changes and provide useful new or updated opportunities with context and contact routes. | Real-time alerts, a fixed cadence, proprietary feeds, automation already running, exclusivity, or guaranteed opportunity volume unless approved and supportable. |
| Utility project briefs | Source-cited public project research screened for the client's actual scope and territory. Client owns procurement decisions, qualification, bids and execution. | An open bid when already awarded, buyer interest, bonding approval, a won contract, or meeting-based billing. |
| Other explicitly selected offer | Write the supported deliverable and mechanism in buyer language, including local appointment acquisition or clinic phone assistance when that is the actual brief. | A borrowed mechanism, guarantee or billable event merely because it appears in another niche's example. |

## A preview list is not automatically the service

In managed outreach, `Want me to put together a short list and send it over?` previews targeting. Email 2 should still communicate the managed service rather than accidentally selling names in a CSV.

In a research/handoff offer, the opportunity context, named contact route and reason to act may be the paid product. An initial short list shows the judgment behind deeper handoffs and continuing monitoring. Do not append `and book the calls` out of habit. For actual list or detailed-handoff fulfillment, use `$signal-opportunity-handoffs` at `a private path (not included)`; do not automatically perform fulfillment during an email-writing request.

Do not pitch generic `research` as the main benefit when the useful output can be named. Explain a prospect-relevant chain in a few ordinary sentences: monitor an event, identify who it could affect, assess plausible fit/timing, and supply the appropriate contact and reason to reach out. Keep fact, inference and actual buyer interest separate. For example, a project announcement may suggest accommodation demand; it is not a confirmed accommodation request.

## New offers and broad fit

A supplied opportunity-research brief is enough to begin local copy planning. If GetLeads or another source supplies a broad cohort, assess what the company sells and whether the proposed work could create a useful buyer/project handoff. Failure to match an older managed-meetings profile is not automatically failure to fit the new product. Conversely, personalized wording cannot fix an unrelated service, wrong identity, no plausible customer route, or an unsupported fulfillment promise.

Do not invent a per-opportunity payment rule, continuous-monitoring cadence or guarantee for a new offer merely to satisfy an email framework. The September 30 E1 preference calls for an explicit short guarantee/payment line: if approved, include it; if missing or conflicting, disclose the gap in the local review using the guarantee-slot rule (private reference, not included) and draft the supported parts. This is not authority to change a fixed fee into pay-only-on-results. Before canonical batch readiness, select or approve a versioned offer profile. Preserve existing snapshots and apply the actual CLI state and mutation gates.

## Commercial compression and exceptions

Use the shortest sentence that preserves the approved obligation, not the shortest sentence that sounds most appealing. A qualified research candidate is different from a qualified attended conversation. A free preview is different from a no-upfront-payment contract. A replacement remedy is different from no payment being due.

Approved copy in a historical campaign is evidence of its wording and scope, not automatic authorization for a different offer. If historical copy and the selected contract disagree, surface the exact mismatch rather than choosing whichever reads better.

Some numbers convey the offer rather than an internal delivery quota. For example, the expressly approved installed-clinic-assistant evaluation can mention 30 days and the keep-or-decline payment condition. That exception applies to that product only. Do not invent clinical claims, HIPAA certification, revenue or staffing savings. Non-medical home-care conversations are a different offer and must not inherit the clinic trial.
