# The Account Profile questions

Source: GitLab handbook, [Account Planning](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/sales/account-planning/#account-profile), "Account Profile" section. Accessed 2026-10-10. The handbook is the authority; the wording below is a close paraphrase, kept in the handbook's order.

The handbook says the Account Profile "is all about the customer (not GitLab)" and tells the AE to "start at a high level." Basic account details already live in Salesforce, so the profile skips firmographics beyond a one-line snapshot.

| # | Question (paraphrased) | Usually answerable from public sources? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What are the company's goals, and which strategic initiatives support each one? Which value drivers matter most, and why? | Yes: 10-K Item 1, shareholder letter, earnings release |
| 2 | Which macro factors (market conditions, competition, regulation and compliance changes) affect the company, and what do they mean for its strategy? | Yes: 10-K Items 1A and 7 |
| 3 | Who are its main competitors, and how does it plan to keep or grow share? | Yes: 10-K Item 1 "Competition" |
| 4 | What are its biggest risks over the short, medium, and long term? | Mostly: 10-K Item 1A, recent 10-Q updates |
| 5 | How does its IT department or strategy support or hinder those objectives? | Partly: 10-K technology sections, cybersecurity Item 1C, job posts |
| 6 | What is its digital transformation strategy, and how does it support the business objectives? | Partly: 10-K, investor day material, newsroom |
| 7 | How is IT perceived as enabling or hindering progress? | Rarely. Usually a discovery question |
| 8 | How does the company view GitLab today: how long a customer, annual spend, which DevOps stages, known expansion areas? | Only from a public GitLab customer story. Otherwise Salesforce and the AE |

## GitLab's four customer value drivers

Source: GitLab handbook, [Command of the Message, Customer Value Drivers](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/sales/command-of-the-message/#customer-value-drivers). Accessed 2026-10-10. Use these exact names:

1. **Increase Operational Efficiencies**: simplify the software toolchain to lower total cost of ownership.
2. **Deliver Better Products Faster**: speed up software delivery to meet business objectives.
3. **Reduce Security and Compliance Risk**: meet internal controls and industry regulation without giving up speed.
4. **Accelerate Cloud Adoption & Transformation**: standardize cloud processes to speed up business outcomes.

The handbook describes value drivers as top-of-mind customer topics that "exist even if GitLab doesn't." So a value driver line must point to something the company itself said or did, not to a GitLab feature.
