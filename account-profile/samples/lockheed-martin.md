# Account Profile: Lockheed Martin Corporation (NYSE: LMT)

Draft for AE review. Built 2026-10-10 from public sources only. Data tier: Green.

**Snapshot:** Global aerospace and defense technology company with four segments (Aeronautics, Missiles and Fire Control, Rotary and Mission Systems, Space). Fiscal 2025 sales of $75.0 billion, about 123,000 employees at December 31, 2025, fiscal year ends December 31. [S1]

## 1. Goals and strategic initiatives
- The stated goal is to deliver capability "quickly, at the needed quantities," while expanding production capacity to meet increased demand. [S1]
- Named initiatives: differentiating technologies, strategic partnerships (including with commercial companies), a multi-year business transformation initiative, fiscal discipline, and workforce. [S1]
- On July 6, 2026 it announced an agreement to buy Ultra Maritime for $3.45 billion; the company expects the deal to close in Q4 2026. [S2, S4]

**Value drivers most likely to matter**
1. Deliver Better Products Faster: speed is part of the stated goal, and the Q2 2026 release cites a counter-drone system taken from concept to live fire testing in 45 days. [S1, S3]
2. Reduce Security and Compliance Risk: it must protect controlled unclassified information under DFARS and submitted its initial CMMC certification in November 2025. [S1]

## 2. Macro factors
- 72% of fiscal 2025 sales came from the U.S. Government; the 10-K flags shutdowns, continuing resolutions and the debt ceiling as funding risks. [S1]
- As of the July 2026 10-Q, the FY 2027 defense topline request sought $1.5 trillion; the company notes it is subject to Congressional appropriation. [S2]
- Tariffs had an impact of about $485 million on fiscal 2025 cash flows, which the company expects to recover in substantial part over time. [S1]

## 3. Competitors and share
- The 10-K names Boeing, General Dynamics, L3Harris Technologies, Northrop Grumman and RTX as primary competitors. [S1]
- It also cites "new entrants, startups and non-traditional defense contractors," and says AI, digital engineering and autonomy are "enabling new factors of competition." [S1]
- Share plan: expand capacity, grow international sales (28% of fiscal 2025 sales), and add capability by acquisition. [S1, S4]

## 4. Biggest risks
- Short term: program execution. A classified Aeronautics program has about $1.8 billion of cumulative losses, and the Q2 2026 10-Q warns of possible further losses. [S2]
- Medium term: concentration. The F-35 program was 27% of fiscal 2025 sales. [S1]
- Long term: the 10-K warns that failing to implement its digital transformation "in a timely manner" could hurt results and competitiveness. [S1]

## 5. How IT supports or hinders the strategy
- The 10-K calls cybersecurity critical to "enabling our digital transformation efforts," with a CISO-led organization owning security strategy and threat response. [S1]
- Compliance load: DFARS controls for CUI, cyber rules flowed down to subcontractors, an initial CMMC submission (November 2025), and ISO 27001 certification. [S1]
- About 72,000 of its approximately 123,000 employees are engineers, scientists and IT professionals. [S1]

## 6. Digital transformation strategy
- The transformation initiative "seeks to significantly enhance our digital infrastructure" for efficiency and collaboration while reducing costs, and needs "substantial investment." [S1]
- The 10-K warns the business may suffer if it cannot integrate AI into internal processes and products in a "timely, cost-effective, compliant and responsible manner." [S1]
- Read: the filings frame the transformation around efficiency and lower cost, while the company goal stresses speed, so a champion will need to say which one the budget is judged on. [S1, S3]
